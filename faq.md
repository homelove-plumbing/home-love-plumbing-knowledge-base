## What is a sewer line emergency?

A sewer line emergency, called a sewer backup or sewer blockage, is a critical malfunction or blockage in a property's wastewater drainage system that stops or severely restricts the flow of sewage away from the building. Severity varies widely. Emergencies range from complete blockages that halt all drainage to partial obstructions that slow flow to a fraction of normal capacity, and they can stem from a clogged main line, a cracked lateral line, root intrusion, grease buildup, or a collapsed pipe section. A sewer line emergency is a type of plumbing emergency, meaning it demands immediate attention rather than a scheduled appointment. Raw sewage backing up into a home or business creates health hazards, structural damage, and code violations that worsen by the hour. ## What is pipe relining?

Pipe relining, called Cured-in-Place Pipe (CIPP), is a trenchless method of repairing damaged pipes by inserting a resin-saturated liner into the existing pipe and curing it in place to form a new structural pipe wall inside the old one, covering sewer lines, drain lines, supply lines and stormwater pipes in diameters from 50 mm to 300 mm without excavating the ground above them.

## What is pipe insulation?

Pipe insulation, called thermal pipe wrap or pipe lagging, is a material applied directly around water supply lines, drain pipes, refrigerant lines, and other plumbing runs. Its purposes are specific: prevent heat loss, limit condensation, protect against freezing, and reduce energy waste. Every section of pipe running through an unconditioned space is a candidate for pipe lagging. This includes attics, crawl spaces, exterior wall cavities, and garages.

## What is water pressure regulator failure?

Water pressure regulator failure, called pressure regulator malfunction, is a plumbing problem in which the regulator valve loses its ability to maintain steady water pressure inside a home's supply lines, producing pressure that is too high, too low or wildly inconsistent at every fixture and appliance connected to the system. The regulator sits on the main supply line where city water enters the building, and when it fails the entire distribution system is affected. The failure impacts every connected outlet instead of a single faucet or zone.

## What is a pressure relief valve?

A pressure relief valve, commonly called a PRV, is a mechanical safety device that automatically opens to discharge excess pressure from a plumbing or heating system when internal pressure climbs above a preset threshold, protecting pipes, water heaters, boilers and connected fixtures from rupture, explosion or catastrophic failure. Requirements can vary and should be confirmed with the local building authority.

## What is backflow prevention device failure?

Backflow Prevention Device Failure, called Backflow Preventer Malfunction, is a plumbing problem in which a backflow prevention assembly stops doing its job of blocking reverse water flow, allowing non-potable water, chemicals or contaminants to move backward into the clean water supply. It is a type of Plumbing Problem that affects residential homes, commercial properties and municipal water systems alike. The failure can be partial, where the device slows but does not stop reverse flow, or complete, where contaminated water moves freely back through the line. ## What is main water shutoff valve?

The main water shutoff valve, called the main stopcock, is a water valve that controls the entire water supply entering a building or property, cutting flow to every fixture, appliance and pipe in the system at once. It is a type of water valve, sitting at the point where the municipal supply line or well line enters the structure, and it is the first and most important isolation point in any home water supply system. A functioning main water shutoff valve is the difference between a burst pipe flooding a home for hours and a homeowner stopping that flood in seconds.

## What is a major water leak?

A major water leak, called a severe or catastrophic leak, is an uncontrolled, significant discharge of water from a damaged plumbing system. It involves a burst pipe, a faulty valve, a cracked fitting, or some combination of these failures releasing water faster than the system can contain it. The volume is large. Unlike a minor drip or slow seep, a major water leak produces enough discharge to cause structural damage, mold growth, and dangerous pressure loss within minutes to hours. ## What is a leaking pipe?

A leaking pipe, called a dripping pipe, is a plumbing condition in which an unintended opening in a pipe wall, joint, seal, or fitting allows water to escape the closed system. The consequences are significant. Water loss, structural damage, mold growth, elevated water bills, and potential contamination of the surrounding environment can all result from a single breach. Leaking pipe is a type of plumbing problem, and it sits at the center of a broad cluster of related conditions ranging from a slow drip at a compression fitting to a burst pipe that floods a room in minutes. Tree root intrusion is the penetration and growth of plant roots into underground plumbing pipes, sewer lines and drain systems, causing partial or complete blockages, structural pipe damage, soil displacement and ongoing flow restriction that worsens over time if left untreated. ## What is an overflowing toilet?

An overflowing toilet, called a toilet overflow, is a plumbing malfunction where water rises above the bowl rim and spills onto the floor. Causes include a clogged drain pipe, a failed flush valve, a blocked overflow tube, a faulty fill valve, or a sewer line obstruction that prevents waste from clearing the trap. It is a type of plumbing problem. This places it in the same category as burst pipes, sewer backups, and drain failures.

## What is a running toilet?

A running toilet, called a leaking toilet, is a plumbing malfunction where water flows continuously or intermittently from the tank into the bowl without a flush being triggered. It wastes water. The cause is typically a faulty float valve, a worn flapper, a misaligned overflow tube, or a combination of these component failures. Running toilets belong to the same category of plumbing malfunction as burst pipes, slab leaks, and failed shut-off valves.

## What is a garbage disposal emergency?

A garbage disposal emergency, called a Food Waste Disposal Failure, is a sudden, serious malfunction of the under-sink food waste unit that renders it inoperable, creates an active water leak, produces dangerous electrical conditions, or generates a blockage severe enough to back sewage into the sink basin. These events are not minor inconveniences. A disposer emergency can escalate within minutes into water damage, mold risk, or a shock hazard if the motor housing cracks and water contacts live wiring.

## What is a gas leak?

A gas leak, called a gas escape, is an unintended escape of fuel gas from a contained plumbing system or appliance. It is a hazardous condition. Gas leaks can affect residential homes, commercial buildings, supply lines, connectors, valves, regulators, and the appliances they feed. Gas leakage is one of the most serious plumbing emergencies a property owner can face, because natural gas is both flammable and, at high concentrations, capable of displacing oxygen in an enclosed space.

## What is pipe corrosion?

Pipe corrosion, called metal pipe oxidation, is the deterioration of metal pipes through chemical reactions between the pipe material and its surrounding environment. Contributing factors include water chemistry, dissolved oxygen, soil conditions, microbial activity, and the pH of the fluid flowing through the system. It weakens pipe walls from the inside out. This deterioration reduces water flow, contaminates drinking water, and eventually causes leaks or full pipe failure. Clearwater's water supply, drawn from sources with variable mineral content and chloramine treatment, creates conditions that accelerate piping deterioration in both older galvanized steel systems and newer copper lines.

## What is structural water damage?

Structural water damage, called building water damage, is deterioration of a building's load-bearing components caused by water ingress. It includes wood decay in floor joists and wall studs, metal corrosion in fasteners and beams, mold growth inside wall cavities, delamination of engineered lumber, and progressive loss of material strength. That strength loss compromises the building's ability to carry its own weight. Structural water damage is a type of water damage. It sits at the severe end of that category because the affected components are the ones the building depends on to stand.
