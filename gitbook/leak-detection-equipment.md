### Measurable attributes of leak detectors

Technicians evaluate several measurable attributes of leak detectors to select the correct tool for each job. The attributes listed here are those examined prior to equipment selection.

- **Sensitivity:** Sensitivity appears as PSI differential or in decibels and defines the smallest leak signal the device can register. High-sensitivity acoustic units can register a leak producing as little as 0.5 dB above ambient noise.

- **Detection sensitivity:** Detection sensitivity for gas-type leak detectors is rated in parts per million (ppm). Professional-grade units typically resolve concentrations below 1 ppm for tracer gases such as hydrogen-nitrogen blends.

- **Range:** Range is the maximum distance in meters between the sensor and the leak source that still produces a reliable reading. Ground microphone systems used on buried mains commonly carry a rated range of 3 to 10 meters depending on soil density and pipe material.

- **Accuracy:** Accuracy is the percentage deviation between the pinpointed location and the actual leak. Well-calibrated correlator systems achieve accuracy within 5 percent of pipe length.

- **Operating frequency range:** Operating frequency range, measured in Hz to kHz, determines which pipe materials and diameters transmit leak noise effectively. Plastic pipe attenuates high-frequency signals faster than copper, so technicians select units tuned to the lower end of the spectrum, often 100 Hz to 1,200 Hz, for PVC and CPVC runs.

- **Power source:** Power source is either a rechargeable lithium-ion battery for field portability or an AC adapter for stationary correlator units used in a van or utility room.

### Common signs that leak detection equipment is needed

Leak detection equipment becomes necessary when a water bill increases without any change in usage, when a water meter continues to move with all fixtures shut off, when unexplained damp spots appear on walls or floors, or when mold odor develops in a room that shows no visible moisture source. A pressure drop test often serves as the first step. The technician isolates the supply line, pressurizes it to its working PSI, and observes the gauge for 15 to 30 minutes. A falling reading confirms a breach somewhere in the system but does not locate it. Acoustic or thermal leak detection equipment enters the workflow at that stage.[Major water leak](major-water-leak.md) situations frequently require this equipment before repair work starts.

### What leak detection equipment is not

Leak detection equipment is not Leak Repair Equipment, Blocking Tools, or Leak Prevention Systems. The distinction remains intentional. Leak prevention systems such as automatic shutoff valves and whole-house flow monitors are installed to stop future leaks from causing damage rather than to locate an existing leak. Professional leak detection equipment fills a distinct role as a diagnostic instrument only. Its output consists of a location rather than a repair. A technician who skips the detection step and opens a wall based on guesswork risks missing the leak entirely and therefore extends both the job duration and the water damage.
